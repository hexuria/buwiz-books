## Starting from a Template

The fastest way to a working chart is to apply a template. On an empty Category Manager, click
**Start from a template**; otherwise open the **⋯** menu and choose **Apply a template…**. Then pick
one:

| Template                    | Accounts | Best for                                        |
| --------------------------- | -------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **General small business**  | 79       | Start here if none of the others fit            |
| **SaaS / startup**          | 87       | Subscription revenue, deferred revenue, hosting |
| **Freelancer / consultant** | 39       | Solo services — the leanest chart               |
| **Retail / e-commerce**     | 86       | Inventory, cost of goods, merchant fees         |

A template does two things: it builds the chart, **and** it fills in every
[category mapping](/admin/mappings), so bills and invoices post correctly from your first
transaction. Setting those 26 mappings by hand is otherwise the slowest part of setup.

### The preview

Nothing is written until you confirm. The preview tells you exactly what will happen — how many
accounts will be **created**, how many existing ones will be **reused**, and how many mappings will
be filled.

Applying a template is **additive and safe on a chart you have already started**:

- Accounts are matched by account number first, then by name and type. A match is reused, never
  duplicated and never overwritten.
- Your names, descriptions, and account numbers are left alone.
- Only genuinely missing accounts are created.
- An account missing a subtype may have one filled in, since mappings and the cash-flow statement
  depend on it.

<Note>
  Applying a second template later is fine — it tops up whatever the first one didn't cover rather
  than replacing your chart.
</Note>

## Extending the Chart with AI

A template covers the common cases; the **⋯ → Customize with AI…** option covers your specific
business. Describe what you do ("we run a specialty coffee shop with a small wholesale roasting arm") and the assistant
drafts additional categories on top of your existing chart.

Two properties are worth knowing:

- **It only adds.** It never renames, re-types, or removes an existing account.
- **Nothing is applied automatically.** The result is a draft proposal. You review it under
  **AI Proposals** and choose what to keep — your chart is unchanged until you approve.

<Tip>
  Apply a template first. The assistant extends an existing chart rather than creating one from
  nothing, so it produces better suggestions with a baseline to work from.
</Tip>

## Creating a New Category

<Steps>
  <Step title="Click + New Category">
    Click the green **+ New Category** button in the top-right corner of the Category Manager.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill in Details">
    | Field | Required | Description | |---|---|---| | **Name** | ✅ | Account name | | **Type** |
    ✅ | Select from the 8 root types | | **Parent** | Optional | Place under a parent category | |
    **Description** | Optional | Explain the account's purpose |
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Click **Create** to add the category. An account number is auto-generated based on the parent
    and type.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Editing a Category

1. Click on any account in the Category Manager list
2. Modify the name, description, or parent assignment
3. Click **Save** to apply changes

<Warning>
  Changing an account's type after transactions have been recorded against it may affect your
  financial reports. Proceed with caution.
</Warning>

## Organizing Hierarchy

Accounts are organized in a tree structure:

```
Assets (10000)
├── Bank Accounts (11000)
│   └── Mercury Checking 1231 (11200)
├── Asset Clearing (11500)
├── Accounts Receivable (12000)
│   └── Accounts Receivable - Gross (12010)
├── Prepaid Expenses (13000)
├── Inventory (13500)
└── Fixed Assets (16000)
    └── Computer Assets (16100)
```

To move an account under a different parent:

1. Edit the account
2. Change the **Parent** dropdown to the new parent category
3. Save — the account number will be re-generated

## Deactivating Accounts

Instead of deleting an account, you can deactivate it:

1. Edit the account
2. Set status to **Inactive**
3. The account will be hidden from dropdowns but preserved for historical transactions

<Note>
  Accounts with existing transactions cannot be deleted. Deactivate them instead to keep your audit
  trail intact.
</Note>

## Importing a Chart of Accounts

You can bulk-import accounts from a CSV file:

1. Go to **Settings → Import/Export**
2. Select **Import Chart of Accounts**
3. Upload a CSV with columns: `name`, `type`, `parentName` (optional), `description` (optional)
4. Review the preview and confirm
