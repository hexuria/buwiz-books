## What is a Cash Flow Statement?

The **Cash Flow Statement** shows how cash entered and left your business during a period. Unlike the P&L (which uses accrual accounting), cash flow tracks the actual movement of money.

<img src="/images/financials/cash-flow.png" alt="Cash Flow Statement" />

## Three Sections

### Operating Activities

Cash generated or spent through normal business operations:

- Cash received from customers
- Cash paid to vendors and employees
- Changes in receivables, payables, and other working capital

### Investing Activities

Cash used for or generated from long-term investments:

- Purchasing equipment or property
- Selling investments
- Capital expenditures

### Financing Activities

Cash from or to owners and lenders:

- Loan proceeds or repayments
- Owner contributions or distributions
- Equity transactions

## Report Structure

```
OPERATING ACTIVITIES
  Revenue Collections            $XX,XXX
  Expense Payments             ($XX,XXX)
  ─────────────────────────────
  Net Cash from Operations       $XX,XXX

INVESTING ACTIVITIES
  Asset Purchases              ($XX,XXX)
  Asset Sales                    $XX,XXX
  ─────────────────────────────
  Net Cash from Investing       ($XX,XXX)

FINANCING ACTIVITIES
  Equity Changes                 $XX,XXX
  Loan Activity                ($XX,XXX)
  ─────────────────────────────
  Net Cash from Financing        $XX,XXX

  ═══════════════════════════════
  NET CHANGE IN CASH             $XX,XXX
  Beginning Cash                 $XX,XXX
  ENDING CASH                    $XX,XXX
```

## How It's Calculated

Buwiz Books uses the **indirect method** to derive cash flow from your ledger entries:

| Account Type                                 | Section    | Logic                                |
| -------------------------------------------- | ---------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Revenue, Cost of Revenue, Operating Expenses | Operating  | Net of income statement items        |
| Other Income, Other Expenses                 | Operating  | Non-operating but through operations |
| Fixed Assets, Investments                    | Investing  | Changes in long-term assets          |
| Equity, Long-term Liabilities                | Financing  | Changes in capital structure         |
| Bank/Cash accounts                           | Net Change | Beginning vs ending balances         |

<Tip>
  A business can be **profitable** (positive net income) but **cash-poor** if revenue is tied up in
  receivables. The Cash Flow Statement reveals this disconnect.
</Tip>
