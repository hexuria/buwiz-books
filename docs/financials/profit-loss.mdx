## What is a Profit & Loss Statement?

The **Profit & Loss** (P&L) statement, also known as the **Income Statement**, shows how much revenue the business earned and how much it spent during a specific period. The difference is your **net income** (profit) or **net loss**.

```
  ═══════════════════════════════
  GROSS PROFIT                   $XX,XXX

Operating Expenses
  Rent                           $XX,XXX

  Utilities                      $XX,XXX
  ─────────────────────────────
  Total Operating Expenses       $XX,XXX

  ═══════════════════════════════
  OPERATING INCOME               $XX,XXX

Other Income / (Expenses)
  Interest Income                $XX,XXX
  Interest Expense              ($XX,XXX)
  ─────────────────────────────
  Total Other                   ($XX,XXX)

  ═══════════════════════════════
  NET INCOME                     $XX,XXX
```

## Key Metrics

| Metric               | Formula                                          | Meaning                              |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| **Gross Profit**     | Revenue − Cost of Revenue                        | Profitability before operating costs |
| **Operating Income** | Gross Profit − Operating Expenses                | Profitability from core operations   |
| **Net Income**       | Operating Income + Other Income − Other Expenses | Bottom-line profitability            |

## Period Comparison

Enable comparison to see how your P&L has changed:

| Mode             | Compares Against                                              |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Prior Period** | The equivalent prior period (e.g., last month vs. this month) |
| **Prior Year**   | Same period in the previous year                              |

Comparison columns show:

- The prior period's amounts
- Dollar change ($ difference)
- Percentage change (% difference)

<Tip>Use the Prior Year comparison for seasonal businesses to compare like-for-like periods.</Tip>
