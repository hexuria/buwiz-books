## What are Category Mappings?

<img src="/images/admin/mappings.png" alt="Category Mappings" />

**Category Mappings** allow you to connect external data sources and system primitives to specific accounts in your Category Manager. This ensures that when financial transactions are recorded (like a bank feed sync, a paid invoice, or a created bill), they are automatically booked to the correct ledger accounts without manual intervention.

To manage mappings, navigate to **Mappings** from the bottom of the main sidebar.

## Tabs Overview

The Mappings page is divided into five main sections:

### 1. Banks & Cards

Map connected bank accounts and credit cards to their corresponding **Asset** or **Liability** accounts.

- **Example**: Map your "Mercury Checking" connection to the `11000 - Bank Accounts` category.
- When journal entries are created for deposits or withdrawals from Mercury, they'll post directly to this category.

### 2. Bills

Fifteen rows drive where bill line items land. **Accounts Payable** and **Default Expense** are the
two that everything falls back on; the rest let a bill line pick a specific expense account by
category instead.

| Row                                  | What posts here                                                |
| ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Accounts Payable**                 | The liability where outstanding bills are tracked until paid   |
| **Default Expense**                  | Fallback for any line item without a more specific category    |
| **Facilities**                       | Rent, utilities, building costs                                |
| **General Operations**               | Day-to-day operating costs that fit nowhere more specific      |
| **Insurance**                        | Business insurance premiums                                    |
| **Supplies & Materials**             | Consumables and materials                                      |
| **Professional Fees**                | Legal, accounting, consulting                                  |
| **Business Applications & Software** | Software subscriptions and licences                            |
| **Payment Processing Fees**          | Merchant and processor fees                                    |
| **Hosting Fees**                     | Infrastructure and hosting                                     |
| **Cost of Goods**                    | Direct costs of goods sold                                     |
| **Taxes**                            | Tax expense                                                    |
| **Interest Expense**                 | Interest paid on debt                                          |
| **Other Miscellaneous Expenses**     | Non-operating odds and ends                                    |
| **Other Current Liabilities**        | Bill amounts that settle to a liability rather than an expense |

### 3. Invoices

Five rows configure how invoice revenue flows into your ledger. The last three are what make
discounted and taxed invoices post correctly — an invoice carrying sales tax or a discount **cannot
be posted** while its row is unmapped.

| Row                     | What posts here                                                         |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Accounts Receivable** | The asset where outstanding invoices sit until they are paid            |
| **Default Revenue**     | Fallback revenue category for invoice line items                        |
| **Sales Tax Payable**   | The liability credited for tax charged on an invoice                    |
| **Refunds & Discounts** | The contra-revenue account debited for invoice discounts                |
| **Payment Clearing**    | The clearing account a payment passes through before it lands in a bank |

### 4. Connections

Manage third-party integrations (like Payroll processors or external Expense management tools) and map them to their respective categories.

- **Example**: Map an external "Gusto" payroll connection to your `Salaries & Wages` expense category.
- **Example**: Map "Stripe" to your `Payment Processor Fees` category.

### 5. Parties

Set default category assignments for transactions associated with specific Vendors or Customers.

_Note: Party-level mappings currently override general system defaults, ensuring that a transaction from "AWS" always hits "Software Subscriptions" regardless of the source._

## Filling Everything at Once

You do not have to set 26 rows by hand. Applying a chart-of-accounts template from the
**Category Manager** creates the accounts **and** writes every Bills, Invoices, and Banks & Cards
mapping in the same step — see [Managing Categories](/accounts/managing-categories).

This is the recommended way to start. Hand-mapping is for adjusting individual rows afterwards, or
for the Connections and Parties tabs, which depend on your own integrations and are not covered by a
template.

### Spotting unmapped rows

A row with no resolvable account is what makes a bill or invoice fail at posting time, so each tab
shows an amber banner counting them:

> **3 of 15 still have no account**
> Insurance, Hosting Fees, Taxes. Anything that posts through these will fail until they are set —
> applying a chart-of-accounts template fills them all in.

The banner disappears once every row on that tab resolves to an active account.

<Warning>
  A mapping only counts if it points at an **active** account of the right type. Deactivating an
  account that a mapping depends on puts that row back into the unmapped banner.
</Warning>

## Creating and Editing Mappings

### How to map an item:

1. Select the relevant tab (e.g., **Banks & Cards**).
2. Locate the item you want to map (e.g., your connected checking account).
3. Click the dropdown beneath the item to open the category combobox.
4. Search for the desired Ledger Category and select it.
5. The change is saved automatically and applies immediately to future transactions.

### Creating Categories Inline

If the category you need doesn't exist yet, you can create it directly from the Mappings page:

1. Under the unmapped item, instead of opening the combobox, click **Create "Suggested Name"**.
2. Buwiz Books will immediately create the required category under the correct account type (e.g., an Asset for a bank account) and automatically map the connection to it.

---

<Note>
  Mappings do **not** retroactively change past transactions. They only apply to new transactions
  generated after the rule is configured.
</Note>
